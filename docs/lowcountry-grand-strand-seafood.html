Lowcountry & Grand Strand seafood

18 places for the coast's own cooking: shrimp and grits, she-crab soup, steam pots and oyster roasts, and Murrells Inlet's fried creek shrimp, each checked in fall 2026 against a 2025–26 source.

Shrimp, crab and oysters

From Hilton Head to Little River, the coast cooks what comes out of its creeks and sounds: shrimp over stone-ground grits, she-crab soup finished with a little sherry, oysters roasted over a fire and shoveled onto the table, steam pots, and the lightly fried Calabash-style shrimp of Murrells Inlet and the Grand Strand. We don't vouch for where any restaurant's shrimp comes from: ask. Under a state law in effect from October 28, 2026, South Carolina restaurants that serve imported shrimp must say so.

The list at a glance

Towns with the most: Charleston (6), Myrtle Beach (3), Murrells Inlet (3), Mount Pleasant (2), Isle of Palms (1), Hilton Head Island (1), Rock Hill (1), Florence (1).

Our method: a place made the list only if a page names it for this food (the state tourism office's Discover South Carolina, local press or the place's own site) and something from 2025 or 2026 shows it's open: a dated page, the place's own site with its current hours when we looked, or a 2025–26 SC Department of Agriculture inspection. Its street address had to match our records. Review sites played no part, and places run by region and town rather than by anybody's score. Hours change, so check before you go.
