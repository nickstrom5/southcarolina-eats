Built around how South Carolina eats

Star ratings won't tell you which pit still cooks whole hogs over coals, or where to find she-crab soup on a Tuesday. South Carolina Eats is organized the way people here eat: whole-hog and mustard barbecue, Lowcountry and Grand Strand seafood, Gullah Geechee cooking and meat-and-threes and drive-ins, each place checked in fall 2026 against a 2025–26 source. Every place we matched to an SCDA permit shows its latest posted grade, never a grade from us, and Apple Maps' live card supplies ratings, hours and photos.

Free, with no catch

The app costs nothing, shows no ads and sells nothing inside, and there's no account to make. The lists ship inside the app, so they open right away and keep working when the signal drops out on a back road or a barrier island.

What stays on your phone

South Carolina Eats collects nothing about you: no sign-in, no analytics, no ads. Location, if you turn it on, only puts the nearest places first, and your saved places are kept on the phone or iPad. The website has no cookies and no trackers. The privacy policy has the details.

Questions

Is South Carolina Eats free?

Completely: no price, no ads, no in-app purchases and no account to create. It's an iPhone and iPad app on its way to the App Store, with no Android version yet; until it's out, the web search here works in any browser.

Where do the lists come from?

Two kinds of source. The whole-hog and mustard barbecue, Lowcountry and Grand Strand seafood, Gullah Geechee cooking and meat-and-threes and drive-ins lists are our own research from fall 2026: a page that names each place for the food (Discover South Carolina, local press or the place's own site), and something from 2025 or 2026 that shows it's open. The wider directory of 15,176 restaurants starts from Overture Maps' open place data, keeps the kinds of listing that proved reliable against the SC Department of Agriculture's statewide permit list, and adds permitted restaurants from that list.

Where can I get whole-hog barbecue?

Our barbecue list has 17 hand-checked joints, from whole hogs cooked over coals to the Midlands' mustard sauce and hash over rice. Many old pits open only a few days a week, so check before you drive. In the web search, the barbecue list puts the nearest first once you share your location.

How do I check a South Carolina restaurant's inspection grade?

The SC Department of Agriculture inspects restaurants statewide and posts a letter grade, A, B or C, after each inspection. For each place we matched to an SCDA permit, the app and this site show its latest posted grade and date as SC Food Grades publishes them, with its recent inspections and a link to each report. We compute no grade or score of our own.

Does it show ratings and reviews?

Not from us. Tapping a place opens Apple Maps' own card inside the app, with Apple's current ratings, hours, photos and directions. None of our lists is sorted by anyone's rating.

Does it need my location?

No. Location only matters for putting the nearest places first. If you share it, it stays on your iPhone or iPad and never reaches us, and the app works fine without it. On this website, Near me reads your location inside your browser and nowhere else.

A place has closed, or something's wrong. How do I tell you?

Write to nick@eatsranked.com with the place's name, its town and what changed. Corrections ship with the next data update.
